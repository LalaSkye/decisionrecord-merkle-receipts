## The three options you named

### MIT
- Shortest, most permissive.
- Anyone can use, copy, change, and redistribute, including in closed-source
  and commercial work.
- Only condition: keep the copyright notice.
- Good when the goal is "let people learn from and reuse this freely".

### Apache-2.0
- Also permissive, slightly longer.
- Same broad freedoms as MIT, plus:
  - an explicit patent grant (protects users from patent claims by contributors)
  - a NOTICE mechanism for attribution
- Good when there may be patent-sensitive material, or institutional use.

### No licence yet
- Default copyright applies: others have no granted right to reuse it.
- Honest holding position while the decision is open.
- Costs nothing to decide later.

## Morpheus recommendation (NON_EXEC)

For a small, inspectable demonstrator whose purpose is "anyone can run it
and see the stop": **MIT**.

It matches the claim boundary — minimal, no overclaim, easy to read.

Apache-2.0 is the better pick only if patent protection or formal NOTICE
attribution matters to you.
